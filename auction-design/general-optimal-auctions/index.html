General Optimal Auctions

This is the second page of a two-page overview of Myerson (1981)'s "Optimal Auction Design." This page will use results discussed in Regular Optimal Auctions, so it is recommended to read that page first.

In the single-item auction environment, Myerson (1981) gives the following result:

Theorem (): In general, a BIC, IR, revenue-maximizing mechanism is a second-price auction in ironed virtual values \(\bar{\phi}_i\) with reserve values \(r_i=\inf\{t\in T_i\mid\bar{\phi}_i(t)\geq 0\}\), \(i=1,\ldots,n\).

For each buyer \(i\) and fixed reports \(v_{-i}\), denote his "second price" as

\[ x_i(v_{-i})=\inf\left\{t\in T_i\ \middle| \begin{array}{l} \bar{\phi}_i(t)\geq 0,\\ \bar{\phi}_i(t)\gt \bar{\phi}_j(v_j)\text{, for every }j\lt i,\\ \bar{\phi}_i(t)\geq\bar{\phi}_j(v_j)\text{, for every }j\gt i \end{array} \right\}. \]

Denote the set of potential winners as

\[ W(v)=\left\{i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}\ \middle| \begin{array}{l} \bar{\phi}_i(v_i)\geq 0,\\ \bar{\phi}_i(v_i)=\max\{\bar{\phi}_j(v_j)\mid j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}\} \end{array} \right\}. \]

Then, equivalently, \((q^*,p^*)\) is an optimal mechanism where for \(i=1,\ldots,n\),

\[ q_i^*(v)=\begin{cases} 1 & \text{if }W(v)\ne\varnothing\text{ and }i=\min W(v),\\ 0 & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}, \qquad p_i^*(v)=\begin{cases} x_i(v_{-i}) & \text{if }q_i^*(v)=1,\\ 0 & \text{if }q_i^*(v)=0 \end{cases}. \]

Ironed virtual values

For each buyer \(i\), denote his virtual value in quantiles as

\[ h_i(\rho)=\phi_i(F_i^{-1}(\rho)) =F_i^{-1}(\rho)-\frac{1-\rho}{f_i(F_i^{-1}(\rho))}, \qquad \rho\in[0,1]. \]

The inverse of \(F_i\) exists, because we assume \(f_i\) is continuous and strictly positive. Next, define its integral

\[ H_i(\rho)\mathrel{:=}\int_0^\rho h_i(t)\,dt. \]

Then, let \(G_i\) be the greatest convex lower bound of \(H_i\). That is, \(G_i\) is the greatest convex function that lies below \(H_i\). Equivalently,

\[ G_i(\rho)=\min\left\{ \omega H_i(t_1)+(1-\omega)H_i(t_2)\ \middle| \begin{array}{l} \omega,t_1,t_2\in[0,1],\\ \omega t_1+(1-\omega)t_2=\rho \end{array} \right\}. \]

We extend its derivative to the whole of \([0,1]\) using the right derivative at \(0\) and at interior points, and the left derivative at \(1\), denoting this extension by

\[ g_i(\rho)\mathrel{:=}\begin{cases} G'_{i,+}(\rho) & \text{if }\rho\in[0,1),\\ G'_{i,-}(1) & \text{if }\rho=1 \end{cases}. \]

Finally, buyer \(i\)'s ironed virtual value is

\[ \bar{\phi}_i(v_i)=g_i(F_i(v_i)). \]

Importantly, since \(G_i\) is convex, \(g_i\) is weakly increasing, and therefore \(\bar{\phi}_i\) is also weakly increasing. If \(\phi_i\) is already weakly increasing, then \(G_i=H_i\) and \(\bar{\phi}_i=\phi_i\). So, in a regular environment, ironed virtual values are the same as the virtual values.

Expected revenue

Starting with our previous expression for expected revenue from a BIC, IR mechanism with allocation rule \(q\) (see Expected revenue on Regular Optimal Auctions), we can rewrite it to include our ironed virtual values:

\[ \begin{aligned} \mathbb{E}\!\left[\sum_{i=1}^n p_i(V)\right] &=-\sum_{i=1}^n U_i(\underline{v}_i) +\mathbb{E}\!\left[\sum_{i=1}^n q_i(V)\,\phi_i(V_i)\right]\\ &=-\sum_{i=1}^n U_i(\underline{v}_i) +\mathbb{E}\!\left[\sum_{i=1}^n q_i(V) \left(\bar{\phi}_i(V_i)+\left(\phi_i(V_i)-\bar{\phi}_i(V_i)\right)\right)\right]\\ &=-\sum_{i=1}^n U_i(\underline{v}_i) +\mathbb{E}\!\left[\sum_{i=1}^n q_i(V)\,\bar{\phi}_i(V_i)\right] +\sum_{i=1}^n\mathbb{E}\!\left[q_i(V)\left(\phi_i(V_i)-\bar{\phi}_i(V_i)\right)\right]. \end{aligned} \]

Denoting \(D_i(v_i)=H_i(F_i(v_i))-G_i(F_i(v_i))\), we can rewrite buyer \(i\)'s term in the final summation as:

\[ \begin{aligned} \mathbb{E}\!\left[q_i(V)\left(\phi_i(V_i)-\bar{\phi}_i(V_i)\right)\right] &=\mathbb{E}\!\left[Q_i(V_i)\left(\phi_i(V_i)-\bar{\phi}_i(V_i)\right)\right] \text{, by the law of iterated expectations}\\ &=\int_{\underline{v}_i}^{\overline{v}_i} Q_i(v_i)\left(\phi_i(v_i)-\bar{\phi}_i(v_i)\right)f_i(v_i)\,dv_i\\ &=\int_{\underline{v}_i}^{\overline{v}_i} Q_i(v_i)\left(h_i(F_i(v_i))-g_i(F_i(v_i))\right)f_i(v_i)\,dv_i\\ &=\int_{\underline{v}_i}^{\overline{v}_i}Q_i(v_i)D_i'(v_i)\,dv_i\text{, by the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus}\\ &=\int_{\underline{v}_i}^{\overline{v}_i}Q_i(v_i)\,dD_i(v_i)\text{, by the absolute continuity of }D_i\\ &=\left[Q_i(v_i)D_i(v_i)\right]_{\underline{v}_i}^{\overline{v}_i}-\int_{\underline{v}_i}^{\overline{v}_i}D_i(v_i)\,dQ_i(v_i)\text{, by Stieltjes integration by parts}. \end{aligned} \]

For the fourth equality involving the FTC, note that when \(D_i\) is differentiable at \(v_i\in[\underline{v}_i,\overline{v}_i]\), we have

\[ \begin{aligned} D_i'(v_i) &=\frac{d}{dv_i}\left(H_i(F_i(v_i))-G_i(F_i(v_i))\right)\\ &=\left(h_i(F_i(v_i))-g_i(F_i(v_i))\right)f_i(v_i)\text{, by the chain rule}. \end{aligned} \]

We can use the FTC because \(D_i\) is absolutely continuous (see Author Insights for justification).
