The First-Price Auction Equilibrium

In a first-price auction, the highest bidder wins and pays his bid.

In the IPV environment, we have the following result:

Proposition: For each buyer \(i\), let \(Y_i=\max\{V_j\mid j\neq i\}\) When buyers are symmetric, it is an equilibrium for them to bid according to

\(\displaystyle \beta^{\mathrm{I}}(v_i)=\mathbb{E}\!\left[Y_i\mid v_i \gt Y_i\right]\)2.

In the interactive demo, take the perspective of buyer \(i\) and consider different bids in response to the other buyers using this strategy. Here, \(V_i=\underline{v}+(\overline{v}-\underline{v})Z_i\), where \(Z_i\overset{\mathrm{i.i.d.}}{\sim}\mathrm{Beta}(\alpha,\beta)\), \(i=1,\ldots,n\). When \(\alpha=\beta=1\), values are \(\mathrm{Uniform}[\underline{v},\overline{v}]\). Choose your value \(v_i\) or randomly draw it. Then, propose a bid \(x_i\) and observe how changing it affects your expected payoff.

Proof of the proposition

Suppose buyers share a value distribution \(F=F_1=\cdots=F_n\). Recall that \(F_i\) has support on \([\underline{v}_i,\overline{v}_i]\), and call the common support \([\underline{v}, \overline{v}]) Fix buyer \(i\) and suppose all other buyers follow \(\beta^{\mathrm{I}}\). Let \(G(t)=F(t)^{n-1}\) denote the distribution function of \(Y_i\), and let \(g\) denote its density.

First, notice that it is not optimal to bid above \(\beta^{\mathrm{I}}(\overline{v})\), since, by assumption, no other buyer will bid above \(\beta^{\mathrm{I}}(\overline{v})\). Similarly, there is no gain from bidding below \(\beta^{\mathrm{I}}(\underline{v})=\underline{v}\). Because \(\beta^{\mathrm{I}}\) is continuous and strictly increasing, every remaining bid can be written as \(\beta^{\mathrm{I}}(v_i')\) for some \(v_i'\in[\underline{v},\overline{v}]\). Then, let's compare bidding \(\beta^{\mathrm{I}}(v_i)\) with bidding \(\beta^{\mathrm{I}}(v_i')\). Denote buyer \(i\)'s expected payoff from bidding some amount \(x_i\in[\underline{v},\beta^{\mathrm{I}}(\overline{v})]\) by \(U_i(v_i,x_i)\). We have

\(\displaystyle U_i \!\left(v_i,\beta^{\mathrm{I}}(v_i')\right) \) \(\displaystyle =\) \(\displaystyle \underbrace{G(v_i')}_{\text{probability of winning}}\times \underbrace{\left(v_i-\beta^{\mathrm{I}}(v_i')\right)}_{\text{payoff upon winning}} + \underbrace{(1-G(v_i'))}_{\text{probability of losing}}\times \underbrace{0}_{\text{payoff upon losing}} \) \(\displaystyle =\) \(\displaystyle G(v_i')\left(v_i-\beta^{\mathrm{I}}(v_i')\right) \) \(\displaystyle =\) \(\displaystyle G(v_i')v_i-G(v_i')\mathbb{E}\!\left[Y_i\mid Y_i\leq v_i'\right] \) \(\displaystyle =\) \(\displaystyle G(v_i')v_i - G(v_i')\frac{\mathbb{E}\!\left[Y_i \cdot \mathbb{1}\{Y_i\leq v_i'\}\right]}{\Pr(Y_i\leq v_i')} \) \(\displaystyle =\) \(\displaystyle G(v_i')v_i - G(v_i')\frac{\int_{\underline{v}}^{\overline{v}} t g(t) \cdot \mathbb{1}\{t\leq v_i'\}\,dt}{G(v_i')} \) \(\displaystyle =\) \(\displaystyle G(v_i')v_i - \int_{\underline{v}}^{\overline{v}} t g(t) \cdot \mathbb{1}\{t\leq v_i'\}\,dt \) \(\displaystyle =\) \(\displaystyle G(v_i')v_i-\int_{\underline{v}}^{v_i'} t g(t)\,dt \) \(\displaystyle =\) \(\displaystyle G(v_i')v_i - \left(G(v_i')v_i' - G(\underline{v})\underline{v} - \int_{\underline{v}}^{v_i'} G(t)\,dt\right)\text{, by integration by parts}\) \(\displaystyle =\) \(\displaystyle G(v_i')v_i - G(v_i')v_i' + 0\cdot \underline{v} + \int_{\underline{v}}^{v_i'} G(t)\,dt \) \(\displaystyle =\) \(\displaystyle G(v_i')v_i-G(v_i')v_i'+\int_{\underline{v}}^{v_i'} G(t)\,dt \) \(\displaystyle =\) \(\displaystyle G(v_i')(v_i-v_i')+\int_{\underline{v}}^{v_i'} G(t)\,dt. \)

Therefore, the difference in expected payoff between bidding \(\beta^{\mathrm{I}}(v_i)\) and bidding \(\beta^{\mathrm{I}}(v_i')\) for buyer \(i\) is

\(\displaystyle U_i \!\left(v_i,\beta^{\mathrm{I}}(v_i)\right)-U_i \!\left(v_i,\beta^{\mathrm{I}}(v_i')\right) \) \(\displaystyle =\) \(\displaystyle \left[G(v_i)(v_i-v_i)+\int_{\underline{v}}^{v_i}G(t)\,dt\right]-\left[G(v_i')(v_i-v_i')+\int_{\underline{v}}^{v_i'}G(t)\,dt\right] \) \(\displaystyle =\) \(\displaystyle G(v_i')(v_i'-v_i)+\int_{v_i'}^{v_i}G(t)\,dt. \) \(\displaystyle =\) \(\displaystyle \int_{v_i'}^{v_i}\left(G(t)-G(v_i')\right)\,dt. \)

Because \(G\) is weakly increasing, this difference is weakly positive for every \(v_i'\in[\underline{v},\overline{v}]\). If \(v_i'\leq v_i\), the integrand is weakly positive, and the limits are in increasing order; if \(v_i'\gt v_i\), the integrand is weakly negative, and the limits are in decreasing order. Thus, given that buyers \(j\neq i\) bid according to \(\beta^{\mathrm{I}}\), it is optimal for buyer \(i\) to also bid according to \(\beta^{\mathrm{I}}\). \(\blacksquare\)

Notes

  1. We have not shown this equilibrium is unique, but a little more work shows that it is the unique symmetric, increasing equilibrium. See , sec. 2.3, pp. 14–16. If we do not restrict to symmetric, increasing equilibria, even more work is required.
  2. At \(v_i=\underline{v}\), since the conditioning event has probability zero, we must explicitly define \(\beta^{\mathrm{I}}(\underline{v})=\underline{v}\). The same issue appears when using the conditional expectation in the proof, but notice that a bidder who bids \(\beta^{\mathrm{I}}(\underline{v})=\underline{v}\) will never win so will always get a payoff of 0. Thus, the final difference is still weakly positive. ↩
