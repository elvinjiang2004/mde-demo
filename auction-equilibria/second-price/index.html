The IPV Second-Price Auction Equilibrium

In the IPV environment, an object will be sold via a second-price sealed-bid auction with no reserve price. Each buyer \(i\) privately observes his realized value \(v_i\), and then all buyers simultaneously submit their bids. The highest buyer wins the object, receives his value for it, and pays the highest opposing bid, while losing buyers receive nothing and pay zero. Ties are broken uniformly at random.

Consider buyer 1 with value \(v_1\). Suppose buyers \(2,\ldots,n\) use the symmetric, increasing bidding strategy

\[ \beta^{\mathrm{II}}(v_i)=v_i,\quad i=2,\ldots,n. \]

Proposition: It is optimal for buyer 1 to use \(\beta^{\mathrm{II}}\) regardless of the other buyers' strategies. Thus, truthful bidding is a weakly dominant strategy for every buyer.

Proof of the dominant strategy for buyer 1

Notice from the demo where the loss from underbidding and overbidding comes from. Underbidding causes you to lose in cases where you would have won and received a positive payoff. Overbidding causes you to win in cases where you receive a negative payoff and would have preferred to lose. In fact, we can show that it is a weakly dominant strategy to bid truthfully, that is, to bid your own value, in the second-price auction.

Suppose buyers \(2,\ldots,n\) bid \(x_2,\ldots,x_n\). Let \(p_1=\max\{x_i \mid i=2,\ldots,n\}\). Let \(x^-\) denote an alternative underbid, so \(x^- < v_1\), and let \(x^+\) denote an alternative overbid, so \(v_1 < x^+\). We consider four cases for \(p_1\):

Case 1: \(p_1 < x^- < v_1\). With either bid, buyer 1 wins and gets \(v_1-p_1>0\).

Case 2: \(x^- < p_1 < v_1\). By bidding \(x^-\), buyer 1 loses and gets \(0\), whereas by bidding \(v_1\), he wins and gets \(v_1-p_1>0\).

Case 3: \(v_1 < p_1 < x^+\). By bidding \(x^+\), buyer 1 wins and gets \(v_1-p_1<0\), whereas by bidding \(v_1\), he loses and gets \(0\).

Case 4: \(v_1 < x^+ < p_1\). With either bid, buyer 1 loses and gets \(0\).

Through these four cases, bidding \(v_1\) gives at least as good of an outcome as, and sometimes a better outcome than, the underbid or overbid. It is also easy to verify that this is true in the cases where \(p_1=x^-\), \(p_1=v_1\), and \(p_1=x^+\). Then, since the initial underbid and overbid were chosen arbitrarily, this holds for all underbids and overbids, which is just all alternative bids. Of course, there is no special reason we considered buyer 1, so it is easy to extend this reasoning to all buyers.

Notes

  • IPV stands for independent, private values.
  • Truthful bidding remains a dominant strategy for buyers in the second-price auction under several extensions, such as risk aversion or asymmetric value distributions.
