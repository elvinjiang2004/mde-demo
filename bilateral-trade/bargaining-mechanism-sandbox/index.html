Bargaining Mechanism Sandbox

A seller has an object that she may sell to a buyer. The seller has value \(C \sim F_S(c)\) and the buyer has value \(V \sim F_B(v)\). We assume \(F_S\) has continuous, strictly positive density \(f_S\) on its support \([\underline{c},\overline{c}]\), and, likewise, \(F_B\) has continuous, strictly positive density \(f_B\) on its support \([\underline{v},\overline{v}]\). We also assume the intersection of the supports is also an interval, that is, it is not empty and not a single point. The buyer and seller observe their respective values, \(v\) and \(c\), but not the other's.

A direct bargaining mechanism consists of an allocation rule and two payment rules. Given that the buyer reports value \(v\) and the seller reports value \(c\), the allocation rule \(q(v,c)\) specifies the probability of trade; the buyer's payment rule \(p_B(v,c)\) specifies the amount the buyer must pay; and the seller's payment rule \(p_S(v,c)\) specifies the amount the seller must receive. Notice that we do not impose \(p_B(v,c)=p_S(v,c)\), so the payment need not be directly from the buyer to the seller.

We evaluate this mechanism according to the following criteria:

  1. Incentive Compatibility (IC): In equilibrium, the buyer and seller report truthfully. This has two forms: Bayesian IC (BIC), where truthful reporting is a Bayes–Nash equilibrium, and Dominant Strategy IC (DSIC), where truthful reporting is a dominant-strategy equilibrium.
  2. Individual Rationality (IR): Both the buyer and seller want to participate. This is evaluated at three stages: ex ante, interim, and ex post.
  3. (Weak) Budget Balance (BB): Trade does not need to be subsidized. This is evaluated at two stages: ex ante and ex post.
  4. Efficiency: The mechanism maximizes welfare. This is evaluated at two stages: ex ante and ex post.
