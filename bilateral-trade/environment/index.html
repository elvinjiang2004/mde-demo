Environment

A seller has an object that she may sell to a buyer. The seller and buyer have independent private values \(C\sim F_S\) and \(V\sim F_B\), respectively. We assume that \(F_S\) has a continuous, strictly positive density \(f_S\) on its support \([\underline{c},\overline{c}]\), and that \(F_B\) has a continuous, strictly positive density \(f_B\) on its support \([\underline{v},\overline{v}]\). These distributions are common knowledge. We also assume that the supports overlap on an interval of positive length. The buyer and seller observe their respective values, \(v\) and \(c\), but not the other's.

By the Revelation Principle, to implement any desired outcome rule, we can consider only direct bargaining mechanisms which consist of an allocation rule \(q:T\to[0,1]\), a payment rule for the buyer \(p_B:T\to\mathbb{R}\), and a payment rule for the seller \(p_S:T\to\mathbb{R}\), where \(T=[\underline{v},\overline{v}]\times[\underline{c},\overline{c}]\). As functions of the reported values, the allocation rule specifies the probability that trade occurs, the buyer's payment rule specifies the expected payment from the buyer, and the seller's payment rule specifies the expected payment to the seller.

Define the following properties of a direct bargaining mechanism:

  1. Incentive Compatibility (IC): Truthful reporting by the buyer and seller is an equilibrium. This has two forms: Bayesian IC (BIC), where truthful reporting is a Bayesian Nash equilibrium, and Dominant-Strategy IC (DSIC), where truthful reporting is a dominant strategy equilibrium.
  2. Individual Rationality (IR): Under truthful reporting, both the buyer and seller have a weakly positive payoff at the relevant stage. This is evaluated at three stages: ex ante, interim, and ex post.
  3. (Weak) Budget Balance (BB): Under truthful reporting, trade does not need to be subsidized. This is evaluated at two stages: ex ante and ex post.
  4. Efficiency: Under truthful reporting, the mechanism maximizes welfare. This is evaluated at two stages: ex ante and ex post.

Denote for a buyer with value \(v\) who reports \(v'\), given the seller reports truthfully:

  • \(\displaystyle Q_B(v')=\int_{\underline{c}}^{\overline{c}} q(v',c)\,f_S(c)\,dc\), his interim probability of trade;
  • \(\displaystyle P_B(v')=\int_{\underline{c}}^{\overline{c}} p_B(v',c)\,f_S(c)\,dc\), his interim expected payment to the mechanism;
  • \(\displaystyle U_B(v,v')=v\,Q_B(v')-P_B(v')\), his interim expected payoff;
  • \(\displaystyle \phi_B(v)=v-\frac{1-F_B(v)}{f_B(v)}\), his virtual value.

Denote for a seller with value \(c\) who reports \(c'\), given the buyer reports truthfully:

  • \(\displaystyle Q_S(c')=\int_{\underline{v}}^{\overline{v}} q(v,c')\,f_B(v)\,dv\), her interim probability of trade;
  • \(\displaystyle P_S(c')=\int_{\underline{v}}^{\overline{v}} p_S(v,c')\,f_B(v)\,dv\), her interim expected payment from the mechanism;
  • \(\displaystyle U_S(c,c')=P_S(c')-c\,Q_S(c')\), her interim expected payoff;
  • \(\displaystyle \phi_S(c)=c+\frac{F_S(c)}{f_S(c)}\), her virtual value.

For compactness, we'll write \(U_B(v,v)\) as \(U_B(v)\) and \(U_S(c,c)\) as \(U_S(c)\).

We call an allocation rule \(q\) BIC (respectively, DSIC) implementable if and only if there exist payment rules \(p_B\) and \(p_S\) such that the mechanism \((q,p_B,p_S)\) is BIC (respectively, DSIC).
