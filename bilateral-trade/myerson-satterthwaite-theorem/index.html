The Myerson-Satterthwaite Theorem

A seller has an object that she may sell to a buyer. The seller and buyer have independent private values \(C\sim F_S\) and \(V\sim F_B\), respectively. We assume that \(F_S\) has a continuous, strictly positive density \(f_S\) on its support \([\underline{c},\overline{c}]\), and that \(F_B\) has a continuous, strictly positive density \(f_B\) on its support \([\underline{v},\overline{v}]\). These distributions are common knowledge. We also assume that the supports overlap on an interval of positive length. The buyer and seller observe their respective values, \(v\) and \(c\), but not the other's.

We consider a direct bargaining mechanism consisting of an allocation rule \(q:T\to[0,1]\), a payment rule for the buyer \(p_B:T\to\mathbb{R}\), and a payment rule for the seller \(p_S:T\to\mathbb{R}\), where \(T=[\underline{v},\overline{v}]\times[\underline{c},\overline{c}]\). As functions of the reported values, the allocation rule specifies the probability that trade occurs, the buyer's payment rule specifies the expected payment from the buyer, and the seller's payment rule specifies the expected payment to the seller.

We want a mechanism that satisfies the following properties:

  1. (Bayesian) Incentive Compatibility (IC): Truthful reporting by the buyer and seller is a Bayesian Nash equilibrium.
  2. (Interim) Individual Rationality (IR): In truthful equilibrium, after observing their values, both the buyer and seller have a weakly positive expected payoff.
  3. (Weak, ex-ante)1 Budget Balance (BB): In truthful equilibrium, trade does not need to be subsidized in expectation.
  4. (Ex post) Efficiency: In truthful equilibrium, the mechanism maximizes welfare at every realized value profile.

Theorem (The Myerson-Satterthwaite Theorem): No mechanism satisfies all four properties.

Incentive compatibility, individual rationality, and the payment rule

You may wonder why you do not need to specify the payment rules in addition to your allocation rule, even though a direct bargaining mechanism consists of all three. Before we can answer this fully, let's specify the following quantities.

For a buyer with value \(v\) who reports \(v'\), given the seller reports truthfully:

  • \(\displaystyle Q_B(v')=\int_{\underline{c}}^{\overline{c}} q(v',c)\,f_S(c)\,dc\), his interim probability of trade;
  • \(\displaystyle P_B(v')=\int_{\underline{c}}^{\overline{c}} p_B(v',c)\,f_S(c)\,dc\), his interim expected payment to the mechanism;
  • \(\displaystyle U_B(v,v')=v\,Q_B(v')-P_B(v')\), his interim expected payoff;
  • \(\displaystyle \phi_B(v)=v-\frac{1-F_B(v)}{f_B(v)}\), his virtual value.

For a seller with value \(c\) who reports \(c'\), given the buyer reports truthfully:

  • \(\displaystyle Q_S(c')=\int_{\underline{v}}^{\overline{v}} q(v,c')\,f_B(v)\,dv\), her interim probability of trade;
  • \(\displaystyle P_S(c')=\int_{\underline{v}}^{\overline{v}} p_S(v,c')\,f_B(v)\,dv\), her interim expected payment from the mechanism;
  • \(\displaystyle U_S(c,c')=P_S(c')-c\,Q_S(c')\), her interim expected payoff;
  • \(\displaystyle \phi_S(c)=c+\frac{F_S(c)}{f_S(c)}\), her virtual value.

For compactness, we'll write \(U_B(v,v)\) as \(U_B(v)\) and \(U_S(c,c)\) as \(U_S(c)\). Now, we can rewrite the IC, IR conditions as

  • IC: \(\displaystyle U_B(v)\geq U_B(v,v')\), for all \(v'\in[\underline{v},\overline{v}]\), and \(\displaystyle U_S(c)\geq U_S(c,c')\), for all \(c'\in[\underline{c},\overline{c}]\);
  • IR: \(\displaystyle U_B(v)\geq 0\), for all \(v\in[\underline{v},\overline{v}]\), and \(\displaystyle U_S(c)\geq 0\), for all \(c\in[\underline{c},\overline{c}]\).

We have the following theorem regarding the implementability2 of an allocation rule \(q\):

Theorem: An allocation rule \(q\) is implementable if and only if \(Q_B\) is weakly increasing and \(Q_S\) is weakly decreasing. Furthermore, the payment rules must set interim expected payments as

\[ P_B(v)=vQ_B(v)-U_B(\underline{v})-\int_{\underline{v}}^v Q_B(t)\,dt,\text{ and } P_S(c)=U_S(\overline{c})+\int_c^{\overline{c}} Q_S(t)\,dt+cQ_S(c). \]

Proof: Start with the forward direction. Suppose \(q\) is implementable with \(p_B\) and \(p_S\). For the buyer, for all \(v_1,v_2\in[\underline{v},\overline{v}]\) with \(v_1\lt v_2\), IC gives

\(\displaystyle U_B(v_1)\geq U_B(v_1,v_2)\) \(\displaystyle \text{and}\) \(\displaystyle U_B(v_2)\geq U_B(v_2,v_1)\) \(\displaystyle \iff v_1Q_B(v_1)-P_B(v_1)\geq v_1Q_B(v_2)-P_B(v_2)\) \(\displaystyle \text{and}\) \(\displaystyle v_2Q_B(v_2)-P_B(v_2)\geq v_2Q_B(v_1)-P_B(v_1)\) \(\displaystyle \iff v_1Q_B(v_1)-v_1Q_B(v_2)\geq P_B(v_1)-P_B(v_2)\) \(\displaystyle \text{and}\) \(\displaystyle v_2Q_B(v_2)-v_2Q_B(v_1)\geq P_B(v_2)-P_B(v_1)\) \(\displaystyle \iff v_1(Q_B(v_1)-Q_B(v_2))\geq P_B(v_1)-P_B(v_2)\) \(\displaystyle \text{and}\) \(\displaystyle v_2(Q_B(v_2)-Q_B(v_1))\geq P_B(v_2)-P_B(v_1).\) \(\displaystyle \iff -v_1(Q_B(v_2)-Q_B(v_1))\geq P_B(v_1)-P_B(v_2)\) \(\displaystyle \text{and}\) \(\displaystyle v_2(Q_B(v_2)-Q_B(v_1))\geq P_B(v_2)-P_B(v_1)\) \(\displaystyle \implies (v_2-v_1)(Q_B(v_2)-Q_B(v_1))\) \(\displaystyle \geq\) \(\displaystyle 0.\)

Then, since \(v_2>v_1\), we have \(Q_B(v_2)-Q_B(v_1)\geq 0\), so \(Q_B\) is weakly increasing. For the seller, a similar reasoning shows for all \(c_1,c_2\in[\underline{c},\overline{c}]\) with \(c_1\lt c_2\), IC gives \(-(Q_S(c_2)-Q_S(c_1))\geq 0\), so \(Q_S\) is weakly decreasing.

Additionally, for all \(v\in[\underline{v},\overline{v}]\) and \(c\in[\underline{c},\overline{c}]\), IC gives

\(\displaystyle U_B(v)=\sup\{vQ_B(v')-P_B(v')|v'\in[\underline{v},\overline{v}]\}\) \(\displaystyle \text{and} \) \(\displaystyle U_S(c)=\sup\{P_S(c')-cQ_S(c')|c'\in[\underline{c},\overline{c}]\}.\)

Then, interim truthful utilities are the upper envelopes of a family of affine functions in \(v\) and in \(c\), respectively. The Envelope Theorem gives the integral conditions:

\(\displaystyle U_B(v) = U_B(\underline{v}) + \int_{\underline{v}}^v Q_B(t) \, dt\) \(\displaystyle \text{and} \) \(\displaystyle U_S(c) = U_S(\overline{c}) + \int_c^{\overline{c}} Q_S(t) \, dt. \)

Then solving for the interim expected payments,

\(\displaystyle U_B(\underline{v}) + \int_{\underline{v}}^v Q_B(t)\, dt = v\,Q_B(v)-P_B(v)\) \(\displaystyle \text{and} \) \(\displaystyle U_S(\overline{c}) + \int_c^{\overline{c}} Q_S(t)\, dt = P_S(c)-c\,Q_S(c) \) \(\displaystyle \iff \) \(\displaystyle P_B(v)= v\,Q_B(v)-U_B(\underline{v})-\int_{\underline{v}}^v Q_B(t)\, dt \) \(\displaystyle \text{and} \) \(\displaystyle P_S(c) = U_S(\overline{c}) + \int_c^{\overline{c}} Q_S(t)\, dt+c\,Q_S(c). \)

For the reverse direction, suppose \(q\) is such that \(Q_B\) is weakly increasing and \(Q_S\) is weakly decreasing. Define \(P_B\) and \(P_S\) as above, and set ex-post payments as \(p_B(v,c)=P_B(v)\) and \(p_S(v,c)=P_S(c)\). Let's verify that this mechanism is IC. For a buyer with value \(v\), for all \(v'\in[\underline{v},\overline{v}]\), the difference between the expected payoff from truthfully reporting \(v\) and misreporting \(v'\) is

\(\displaystyle U_B(v) - U_B(v,v') \) \(\displaystyle = \) \(\displaystyle \left[vQ_B(v)-P_B(v)\right] - \left[vQ_B(v')-P_B(v')\right] \) \(\displaystyle \) \(\displaystyle \) \(\displaystyle = \) \(\displaystyle \left[vQ_B(v)-\left(vQ_B(v)-U_B(\underline{v})-\int_{\underline{v}}^v Q_B(t)\, dt\right)\right] - \left[vQ_B(v')-\left(v'Q_B(v')-U_B(\underline{v})-\int_{\underline{v}}^{v'} Q_B(t)\, dt\right)\right] \) \(\displaystyle \) \(\displaystyle \) \(\displaystyle = \) \(\displaystyle U_B(\underline{v}) - U_B(\underline{v}) + \int_{\underline{v}}^v Q_B(t)\, dt - vQ_B(v')+v'Q_B(v')-\int_{\underline{v}}^{v'} Q_B(t)\, dt \) \(\displaystyle \) \(\displaystyle \) \(\displaystyle = \) \(\displaystyle Q_B(v')(v'-v) + \int_{v'}^v Q_B(t)\, dt \).

Because \(Q_B\) is weakly increasing, this difference is weakly positive for all \(v,v'\in[\underline{v},\overline{v}]\). A similar argument gives IC for the seller. Thus, the mechanism is IC. \(\blacksquare\)

Notice in the above payments we are free to choose \(U_B(\underline{v})\) and \(U_S(\overline{c})\) without affecting IC implementability. As long as we choose \(U_B(\underline{v})\geq 0\) and \(U_S(\overline{c})\geq 0\), the mechanism will also be IR, since \(Q_B(v)\geq 0\) and \(Q_S(c)\geq 0\).

Budget balance

Now that we have interim expected payments, we can calculate ex-ante expected payments from the buyer and to the seller directly. First, from the buyer, we can expect to receive

\(\displaystyle \mathbb{E}\!\left[p_B(V,C)\right]\) \(\displaystyle =\) \(\displaystyle \mathbb{E}\!\left[P_B(V)\right]\) \(\displaystyle =\) \(\displaystyle \int_{\underline{v}}^{\overline{v}} P_B(v)\,f_B(v)\,dv\) \(\displaystyle =\) \(\displaystyle \int_{\underline{v}}^{\overline{v}} \left(vQ_B(v)-U_B(\underline{v})-\int_{\underline{v}}^{v} Q_B(t)\,dt\right) f_B(v)\,dv\) \(\displaystyle =\) \(\displaystyle \int_{\underline{v}}^{\overline{v}} \left(vQ_B(v)-U_B(\underline{v})\right) f_B(v)\,dv - \int_{\underline{v}}^{\overline{v}}\int_{\underline{v}}^{v} Q_B(t)\,dt\,f_B(v)\,dv.\)

To compute this, we first simplify the final term.

\(\displaystyle \int_{\underline{v}}^{\overline{v}}\int_{\underline{v}}^{v} Q_B(t)\,dt\,f_B(v)\,dv\) \(\displaystyle =\) \(\displaystyle \int_{\underline{v}}^{\overline{v}}\int_{\underline{v}}^{v} Q_B(t)\,f_B(v)\,dt\,dv\) \(\displaystyle =\) \(\displaystyle \int_{\underline{v}}^{\overline{v}}\int_{t}^{\overline{v}} Q_B(t)\,f_B(v)\,dv\,dt \text{, by Fubini's Theorem}\) \(\displaystyle =\) \(\displaystyle \int_{\underline{v}}^{\overline{v}} Q_B(t)\int_{t}^{\overline{v}} f_B(v)\,dv\,dt\) \(\displaystyle =\) \(\displaystyle \int_{\underline{v}}^{\overline{v}} Q_B(t)\left(\int_{\underline{v}}^{\overline{v}} f_B(v)\,dv-\int_{\underline{v}}^{t} f_B(v)\,dv\right)dt\) \(\displaystyle =\) \(\displaystyle \int_{\underline{v}}^{\overline{v}} Q_B(t)\left(1-F_B(t)\right)dt\) \(\displaystyle =\) \(\displaystyle \int_{\underline{v}}^{\overline{v}} Q_B(v)\left(1-F_B(v)\right)dv.\)

Substituting back into the earlier expected payment calculation,

\(\displaystyle \mathbb{E}\!\left[p_B(V,C)\right]\) \(\displaystyle =\) \(\displaystyle \int_{\underline{v}}^{\overline{v}} vQ_B(v)\,f_B(v)\,dv - \int_{\underline{v}}^{\overline{v}} U_B(\underline{v})\,f_B(v)\,dv - \int_{\underline{v}}^{\overline{v}} Q_B(v)\left(1-F_B(v)\right)dv\) \(\displaystyle =\) \(\displaystyle \int_{\underline{v}}^{\overline{v}} vQ_B(v)f_B(v)\,dv -U_B(\underline{v}) - \int_{\underline{v}}^{\overline{v}} Q_B(v)\frac{1-F_B(v)}{f_B(v)}f_B(v)\,dv\) \(\displaystyle =\) \(\displaystyle -U_B(\underline{v}) + \int_{\underline{v}}^{\overline{v}} Q_B(v)\left(v-\frac{1-F_B(v)}{f_B(v)}\right)f_B(v)\,dv\) \(\displaystyle =\) \(\displaystyle -U_B(\underline{v}) + \int_{\underline{v}}^{\overline{v}} Q_B(v)\,\phi_B(v)\,f_B(v)\,dv\) \(\displaystyle =\) \(\displaystyle -U_B(\underline{v}) + \mathbb{E}\!\left[Q_B(V)\,\phi_B(V)\right],\)

recalling the earlier definition of the buyer's virtual value \(\phi_B(v)\).

For the step involving Fubini's Theorem, note that we assume \(f_B\) is continuous on the compact interval \([\underline{v},\overline{v}]\), and is therefore bounded. Similarly \(Q_B\) is, by definition, bounded. Thus, the integrand is absolutely integrable, permitting the change in integration order.

Let's rewrite the expected value term to be more convenient for later calculations.

\(\displaystyle \mathbb{E}\!\left[Q_B(V)\,\phi_B(V)\right]\) \(\displaystyle =\) \(\displaystyle \int_{\underline{v}}^{\overline{v}} Q_B(v)\,\phi_B(v)\,f_B(v)\,dv\) \(\displaystyle =\) \(\displaystyle \int_{\underline{v}}^{\overline{v}} \int_{\underline{c}}^{\overline{c}} q(v,c)\,f_S(c)\,dc\,\phi_B(v)\,f_B(v)\,dv, \text{ by the definition of } Q_B(v)\) \(\displaystyle =\) \(\displaystyle \int_{\underline{v}}^{\overline{v}}\mathbb{E}\!\left[q(v,C)\right]\,\phi_B(v)\,f_B(v)\,dv\) \(\displaystyle =\) \(\displaystyle \mathbb{E}\!\left[\mathbb{E}\!\left[q(V,C)\mid V\right]\,\phi_B(V)\right]\) \(\displaystyle =\) \(\displaystyle \mathbb{E}\!\left[q(V,C)\,\phi_B(V)\right], \text{ by the law of iterated expectations.}\)

After a similar calculation for the seller, the expected payments from the buyer and to the seller are

\(\displaystyle \mathbb{E}\!\left[p_B(V,C)\right] = -U_B(\underline{v}) + \mathbb{E}\!\left[q(V,C)\,\phi_B(V)\right]\) \(\displaystyle \text{and}\) \(\displaystyle \mathbb{E}\!\left[p_S(V,C)\right] = U_S(\overline{c}) + \mathbb{E}\!\left[q(V,C)\,\phi_S(C)\right].\)

Now, we can rewrite the BB condition as

\(\displaystyle \mathbb{E}\!\left[p_B(V,C)\right] - \mathbb{E}\!\left[p_S(V,C)\right]\) \(\displaystyle =\) \(\displaystyle -U_B(\underline{v}) - U_S(\overline{c}) + \mathbb{E}\!\left[q(V,C)\,(\phi_B(V)-\phi_S(C))\right]\geq 0.\)

Efficiency

The efficient allocation rule specifies trade when \(v\geq c\) and no trade when \(v<c\). That is, \(q^*(v,c)=\mathbb{1}(v\geq c)\) is the efficient allocation rule. This has interim allocation probabilities

\[ Q_B(v)=F_S(v) \quad\text{and}\quad Q_S(c)=1-F_B(c). \]

These are weakly monotone in the right directions, so this allocation rule is IC with the previously derived payment rules. For the best chance of budget balance while preserving IR, let's assign \(U_B(\underline{v})=0\) and \(U_S(\overline{c})=0\). Now it remains to check, is this BB?

First, we express the BB condition for the efficient allocation rule.

\(\displaystyle -U_B(\underline{v}) - U_S(\overline{c}) + \mathbb{E}\!\left[q(V,C)\,(\phi_B(V)-\phi_S(C))\right]\) \(\displaystyle =\) \(\displaystyle \mathbb{E}\!\left[\mathbb{1}(V\geq C)\,(\phi_B(V)-\phi_S(C))\right]\) \(\displaystyle =\) \(\displaystyle \mathbb{E}\!\left[\mathbb{1}(V\geq C)\,\left(V-\frac{1-F_B(V)}{f_B(V)}-\left(C+\frac{F_S(C)}{f_S(C)}\right)\right)\right], \text{ by the definition of } \phi_B \text{ and } \phi_S\) \(\displaystyle =\) \(\displaystyle \mathbb{E}\!\left[\mathbb{1}(V\geq C)\,(V-C)\right]-\mathbb{E}\!\left[\mathbb{1}(V\geq C)\,\frac{1-F_B(V)}{f_B(V)}\right]-\mathbb{E}\!\left[\mathbb{1}(V\geq C)\,\frac{F_S(C)}{f_S(C)}\right]\) \(\displaystyle =\) \(\displaystyle \mathbb{E}\!\left[\mathbb{1}(V\geq C)\,(V-C)\right]-\int_{\underline{v}}^{\overline{v}} \int_{\underline{c}}^{\overline{c}} \mathbb{1}(v\geq c) \frac{1-F_B(v)}{f_B(v)}\,f_S(c)\,f_B(v)\,dc\,dv-\int_{\underline{c}}^{\overline{c}} \int_{\underline{v}}^{\overline{v}} \mathbb{1}(v\geq c) \frac{F_S(c)}{f_S(c)}\,f_B(v)\,f_S(c)\,dv\,dc\) \(\displaystyle =\) \(\displaystyle \mathbb{E}\!\left[\mathbb{1}(V\geq C)\,(V-C)\right]-\int_{\underline{v}}^{\overline{v}} \int_{\underline{c}}^{v} f_S(c)\,dc\,(1-F_B(v))\,dv-\int_{\underline{c}}^{\overline{c}} \int_{c}^{\overline{v}}f_B(v)\,dv\,F_S(c)\,dc\) \(\displaystyle =\) \(\displaystyle \mathbb{E}\!\left[\mathbb{1}(V\geq C)\,(V-C)\right]-\int_{\underline{v}}^{\overline{v}} F_S(v)\left(1-F_B(v)\right)\,dv-\int_{\underline{c}}^{\overline{c}} (1-F_B(c)) F_S(c)\,dc\geq 0\)

Next, we rewrite the first term.

\(\displaystyle \mathbb{E}\!\left[\mathbb{1}(V\geq C)\,(V-C)\right]\) \(\displaystyle =\) \(\displaystyle \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\!\!\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathbb{1}(c\leq v)\,(v-c)\,f_B(v)f_S(c)\,dv\,dc\) \(\displaystyle =\) \(\displaystyle \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\!\!\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left(\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathbb{1}(c\leq t)\,\mathbb{1}(t\leq v)\,dt\right)f_B(v)f_S(c)\,dv\,dc \text{, by the layer-cake representation}\) \(\displaystyle =\) \(\displaystyle \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left(\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathbb{1}(c\leq t)f_S(c)\,dc\right)\left(\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathbb{1}(t\leq v)f_B(v)\,dv\right)dt \text{, by Fubini's Theorem}\) \(\displaystyle =\) \(\displaystyle \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left(\int_{-\infty}^{t}f_S(c)\,dc\right)\left(\int_{t}^{\infty}f_B(v)\,dv\right)dt\) \(\displaystyle =\) \(\displaystyle \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} F_S(t)\left(1-F_B(t)\right)dt.\)

The layer-cake representation for \(v-c\) when \(v\geq c\) is

\[ v-c=\int_c^v dt=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathbb{1}(c\leq t)\,\mathbb{1}(t\leq v)\,dt. \]

Then, we substitute back into the earlier BB condition calculation.

\(\displaystyle \mathbb{E}\!\left[p_B(V,C)\right]-\mathbb{E}\!\left[p_S(V,C)\right]\) \(\displaystyle =\) \(\displaystyle \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} F_S(t)\left(1-F_B(t)\right)\,dt - \int_{\underline{v}}^{\overline{v}} F_S(v)\left(1-F_B(v)\right)\,dv-\int_{\underline{c}}^{\overline{c}} F_S(c)\left(1-F_B(c)\right)\,dc\) \(\displaystyle =\) \(\displaystyle \int_{[\underline{v},\overline{v}]\cup[\underline{c},\overline{c}]}F_S(t)\left(1-F_B(t)\right)dt-\left(\int_{[\underline{v},\overline{v}]}F_S(t)\left(1-F_B(t)\right)dt+\int_{[\underline{c},\overline{c}]}F_S(t)\left(1-F_B(t)\right)dt\right)\) \(\displaystyle =\) \(\displaystyle -\int_{[\underline{v},\overline{v}]\cap[\underline{c},\overline{c}]}F_S(t)\left(1-F_B(t)\right)dt, \text{ by the inclusion-exclusion principle.}\)

Since we assumed strictly positive density on both supports which intersect at an interval of positive length, this remaining term is strictly negative. Thus, the efficient allocation rule is not implementable with IC, IR, and BB.

Notes

  1. Most of the times, the Myerson-Satterthwaite theorem is stated for ex-post BB. show that the two problems are equivalent, that is, if a mechanism is ex-ante BB, then it can be modified to be ex-post BB while preserving IC and IR. ↩
  2. By definition, an allocation rule \(q\) is implementable if and only if there exist payment rules \(p_B\) and \(p_S\) such that the mechanism \((q,p_B,p_S)\) is IC. ↩
