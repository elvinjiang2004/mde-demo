The Myerson-Satterthwaite Theorem

A seller has an object that she may sell to a buyer. The seller has value \(C \sim F_S(c)\) and the buyer has value \(V \sim F_B(v)\). We assume \(F_S\) has continuous, strictly positive density \(f_S\) on its support \([\underline{c},\overline{c}]\), and, likewise, \(F_B\) has continuous, strictly positive density \(f_B\) on its support \([\underline{v},\overline{v}]\). We also assume the intersection of the supports is also an interval, that is, it is not empty and not a single point. The buyer and seller observe their respective values, \(v\) and \(c\), but not the other's.

We want to find a direct bargaining mechanism consisting of an allocation rule and two payment rules \((q(v,c),p_B(v,c),p_S(v,c))\) in this environment that satisfies:

  1. (Bayesian) Incentive Compatibility (IC): Given the seller reports her true value, it is optimal for the buyer to do the same, and vice versa.
  2. (Interim) Individual Rationality (IR): Both the buyer and seller have a weakly positive expected payoff after observing their values.
  3. (Weak, ex-ante) Budget Balance (BB): The trade does not need to be subsidized by an outside party in expectation.
  4. (Ex-post) Pareto Efficiency (PE): The mechanism maximizes welfare at every realized value profile. In other words, the person who values the object most always ends up with it.

The Myerson-Satterthwaite Theorem says that a such a mechanism does not exist. In the interactive demo, design a direct bargaining mechanism and see where it fails. Here, \(V,C \mathrel{\overset{\mathrm{i.i.d.}}{\sim}} \operatorname{Uniform}[0,1]\).

Incentive compatibility, individual rationality, and the payment rule

You may wonder why you do not need to specify the payment rules in addition to your allocation rule, even though a direct bargaining mechanism consists of all three. Before we can answer this fully, let's specify the following quantities.

For a buyer with value \(v\) who reports \(v'\), given the seller reports truthfully:

  • \(\displaystyle Q_B(v')=\int_{\underline{c}}^{\overline{c}} q(v',c)\,f_S(c)\,dc\), his interim probability of trade;
  • \(\displaystyle P_B(v')=\int_{\underline{c}}^{\overline{c}} p_B(v',c)\,f_S(c)\,dc\), his interim expected payment to;
  • \(\displaystyle U_B(v,v')=v\,Q_B(v')-P_B(v')\), his interim expected payoff;
  • \(\displaystyle \phi_B(v)=v-\frac{1-F_B(v)}{f_B(v)}\), his virtual value.

For a seller with value \(c\) who reports \(c'\), given the buyer reports truthfully:

  • \(\displaystyle Q_S(c')=\int_{\underline{v}}^{\overline{v}} q(v,c')\,f_B(v)\,dv\), her interim probability of trade;
  • \(\displaystyle P_S(c')=\int_{\underline{v}}^{\overline{v}} p_S(c',v)\,f_B(v)\,dv\), her interim expected payment from;
  • \(\displaystyle U_S(c,c')=P_S(c')-c\,Q_S(c')\), her interim expected payoff;
  • \(\displaystyle \phi_S(c)=c+\frac{F_S(c)}{f_S(c)}\), her virtual value.

For compactness, we'll write \(U_B(v,v)\) as \(U_B(v)\) and \(U_S(c,c)\) as \(U_S(c)\). Now, we can rewrite the IC, IR conditions as

  • IC: \(\displaystyle U_B(v)\geq U_B(v,v')\), for all \(v'\in[\underline{v},\overline{v}]\), and \(\displaystyle U_S(c)\geq U_S(c,c')\), for all \(c'\in[\underline{c},\overline{c}]\);
  • IR: \(\displaystyle U_B(v)\geq 0\), for all \(v\in[\underline{v},\overline{v}]\), and \(\displaystyle U_S(c)\geq 0\), for all \(c\in[\underline{c},\overline{c}]\).

We have the following theorem regarding the implementability of an allocation rule \(q(v,c)\):

Theorem: An allocation rule \(q(v,c)\) is IC implementable by some payment rules if and only if \(Q_B\) is weakly increasing and \(Q_S\) is weakly decreasing. Furthermore, the payment rules must set interim expected payments as

\[ P_B(v,c)=P_B(\underline{v})+vQ_B(v)-\int_{\underline{v}}^v Q_B(t)\,dt,\text{ and } P_S(v,c)=P_S(\overline{c})+\int_c^{\overline{c}} Q_S(t)\,dt-cQ_S(c). \]

Proof: Start with the forward implication. For the buyer, for all \(v, v'\in[\underline{v},\overline{v}]\), by IC

\(\displaystyle U_B(v) \) \(\displaystyle \geq\) \(\displaystyle U_B(v,v') = vQ_B(v')-P_B(v') \) \(\displaystyle \iff \) \(\displaystyle U_B(v) \) \(\displaystyle =\) \(\displaystyle \max\left\{ vQ_B(v')-P_B(v')| v' \in [\underline{v},\overline{v}] \right\} \)

Then, \(U_B(v)\) is the upper envelope of a family of affine functions\(\implies\) it is convex \(\implies\) it is absolutely continuous \(\implies\) its derivative exists almost everywhere. By the envelope theorem, we can differentiate through the maximum almost everywhere, so \(U_B'(v)=Q_B(v)\) almost everywhere, and so \(Q_B\) is weakly increasing, since \(U_B\) is convex. A similar argument using lower envelopes and concavity shows that \(Q_S\) is weakly decreasing.

Once we have \(Q_B\) and \(Q_S\), Corollary 1 in Milgrom and Segal (2002) states that \(U_B\) and \(U_S\) satisfy the integral conditions

\(\displaystyle U_B(v) = U_B(\underline{v}) + \int_{\underline{v}}^v Q_B(t) \, dt\) \(\displaystyle \text{and} \) \(\displaystyle U_S(c) = U_S(\overline{c}) + \int_c^{\overline{c}} Q_S(t) \, dt. \)

Then solving for the interim expected payments,

\(\displaystyle U_B(\underline{v}) + \int_{\underline{v}}^v Q_B(t)\, dt = v\,Q_B(v)-P_B(v)\) \(\displaystyle \text{and} \) \(\displaystyle U_S(\overline{c}) + \int_c^{\overline{c}} Q_S(t)\, dt = P_S(c)-c\,Q_S(c) \) \(\displaystyle \iff \) \(\displaystyle P_B(v)= v\,Q_B(v)-U_B(\underline{v})-\int_{\underline{v}}^v Q_B(t)\, dt \) \(\displaystyle \text{and} \) \(\displaystyle P_S(c) = U_S(\overline{c}) + \int_c^{\overline{c}} Q_S(t)\, dt+c\,Q_S(c) \)

And we can set ex-post payments as \(p_B(v,c)=P_B(v)\) and \(p_S(v,c)=P_S(c)\).

For the reverse implication, suppose \(Q_B\) is weakly increasing, \(Q_S\) is weakly decreasing, and the payments imply the above interim expected payments. Let's verify that this mechanism is IC. For a buyer with value \(v\), for all \(v'\in[\underline{v},\overline{v}]\), the difference between the expected payoff from truthfully reporting \(v\) and misreporting \(v'\) is

\(\displaystyle U_B(v) - U_B(v,v') \) \(\displaystyle = \) \(\displaystyle \left[vQ_B(v)-P_B(v)\right] - \left[vQ_B(v')-P_B(v')\right] \) \(\displaystyle \) \(\displaystyle \) \(\displaystyle = \) \(\displaystyle \left[vQ_B(v)-\left(vQ_B(v)-U_B(\underline{v})-\int_{\underline{v}}^v Q_B(t)\, dt\right)\right] - \left[vQ_B(v')-\left(v'Q_B(v')-U_B(\underline{v'})-\int_{\underline{v'}}^{v'} Q_B(t)\, dt\right)\right] \) \(\displaystyle \) \(\displaystyle \) \(\displaystyle = \) \(\displaystyle U_B(\underline{v}) - U_B(\underline{v'}) + \int_{\underline{v}}^v Q_B(t)\, dt - vQ_B(v')+v'Q_B(v')-\int_{\underline{v'}}^{v'} Q_B(t)\, dt \) \(\displaystyle \) \(\displaystyle \) \(\displaystyle = \) \(\displaystyle Q_B(v')(v'-v) + \int_{v'}^v Q_B(t)\, dt \).

Because \(Q_B\) is weakly increasing, this difference is weakly positive for all \(v,v'\in[\underline{v},\overline{v}]\). A similar argument gives the seller's IC condition. Thus, the mechanism is IC. \(\blacksquare\)

Notice in the above payments we are free to choose \(U_B(\underline{v})\) and \(U_S(\overline{c})\) without affecting IC implementability. As long as we choose \(U_B(\underline{v})\geq 0\) and \(U_S(\overline{c})\geq 0\), the mechanism will also be IR, since \(Q_B(v)\geq 0\) and \(Q_S(c)\geq 0\).

Budget balance

Now that we have interim expected payments, we can calculate ex-ante expected payments from the buyer and to the seller directly. First, from the buyer, we can expect to receive

\(\displaystyle \mathbb{E}\!\left[p_B(v,c)\right]\) \(\displaystyle =\) \(\displaystyle \mathbb{E}\!\left[P_B(v)\right]\) \(\displaystyle =\) \(\displaystyle \int_{\underline{v}}^{\overline{v}} P_B(v)\,f_B(v)\,dv\) \(\displaystyle =\) \(\displaystyle \int_{\underline{v}}^{\overline{v}} \left(vQ_B(v)-U_B(\underline{v})-\int_{\underline{v}}^{v} Q_B(t)\,dt\right) f_B(v)\,dv\) \(\displaystyle =\) \(\displaystyle \int_{\underline{v}}^{\overline{v}} \left(vQ_B(v)-U_B(\underline{v})\right) f_B(v)\,dv - \int_{\underline{v}}^{\overline{v}}\int_{\underline{v}}^{v} Q_B(t)\,dt\,f_B(v)\,dv.\)

To compute this, we first simplify the final term.

\(\displaystyle \int_{\underline{v}}^{\overline{v}}\int_{\underline{v}}^{v} Q_B(t)\,dt\,f_B(v)\,dv\) \(\displaystyle =\) \(\displaystyle \int_{\underline{v}}^{\overline{v}}\int_{\underline{v}}^{v} Q_B(t)\,f_B(v)\,dt\,dv\) \(\displaystyle =\) \(\displaystyle \int_{\underline{v}}^{\overline{v}}\int_{t}^{\overline{v}} Q_B(t)\,f_B(v)\,dv\,dt \text{, by Fubini's Theorem}\) \(\displaystyle =\) \(\displaystyle \int_{\underline{v}}^{\overline{v}} Q_B(t)\int_{t}^{\overline{v}} f_B(v)\,dv\,dt\) \(\displaystyle =\) \(\displaystyle \int_{\underline{v}}^{\overline{v}} Q_B(t)\left(\int_{\underline{v}}^{\overline{v}} f_B(v)\,dv-\int_{\underline{v}}^{t} f_B(v)\,dv\right)dt\) \(\displaystyle =\) \(\displaystyle \int_{\underline{v}}^{\overline{v}} Q_B(t)\left(1-F_B(t)\right)dt\) \(\displaystyle =\) \(\displaystyle \int_{\underline{v}}^{\overline{v}} Q_B(v)\left(1-F_B(v)\right)dv.\)

Substituting back into the earlier expected payment calculation,

\(\displaystyle \mathbb{E}\!\left[p_B(v,c)\right]\) \(\displaystyle =\) \(\displaystyle \int_{\underline{v}}^{\overline{v}} vQ_B(v)\,f_B(v)\,dv - \int_{\underline{v}}^{\overline{v}} U_B(\underline{v})\,f_B(v)\,dv - \int_{\underline{v}}^{\overline{v}} Q_B(v)\left(1-F_B(v)\right)dv\) \(\displaystyle =\) \(\displaystyle \int_{\underline{v}}^{\overline{v}} vQ_B(v)f_B(v)\,dv -U_B(\underline{v}) - \int_{\underline{v}}^{\overline{v}} Q_B(v)\frac{1-F_B(v)}{f_B(v)}f_B(v)\,dv\) \(\displaystyle =\) \(\displaystyle -U_B(\underline{v}) + \int_{\underline{v}}^{\overline{v}} Q_B(v)\left(v-\frac{1-F_B(v)}{f_B(v)}\right)f_B(v)\,dv\) \(\displaystyle =\) \(\displaystyle -U_B(\underline{v}) + \int_{\underline{v}}^{\overline{v}} Q_B(v)\,\phi_B(v)\,f_B(v)\,dv\) \(\displaystyle =\) \(\displaystyle -U_B(\underline{v}) + \mathbb{E}\!\left[Q_B(v)\,\phi_B(v)\right],\)

recalling the earlier definition of the buyer's virtual value \(\phi_B(v)\).

For the step involving Fubini's Theorem, note that we assume \(f_B\) is continuous on the compact interval \([\underline{v},\overline{v}]\), and is therefore bounded. Similarly \(Q_B\) is, by definition, bounded. Thus, the integrand is absolutely integrable, permitting the change in integration order.

Again, a similar calculation gives, for the seller,

\[ \mathbb{E}\!\left[p_S(v,c)\right] = U_S(\overline{c}) + \mathbb{E}\!\left[Q_S(c)\,\phi_S(c)\right]. \]

Now, we can rewrite the BB condition as

\[ \mathbb{E}\!\left[p_B(v,c)\right] - \mathbb{E}\!\left[p_S(v,c)\right] = -U_B(\underline{v}) - U_S(\overline{c}) + \mathbb{E}\!\left[Q_B(v)\,\phi_B(v)\right] - \mathbb{E}\!\left[Q_S(c)\,\phi_S(c)\right]\geq 0. \]

Efficiency

The efficient allocation rule specifies trade when \(v>c\) and no trade when \(v<c\). That is, \(q(v,c)=\mathbb{1}(v>c)\) is the efficient allocation rule. This has interim allocation probabilities

\[ Q_B(v)=P(C\leq v)=F_S(v) \quad\text{and}\quad Q_S(c)=P(V\geq c)=1-F_B(c). \]

These are weakly monotone in the right directions, so this allocation rule is IC implementable. For the best chance of budget balance while preserving IR, let's assign \(U_B(\underline{v})=0\) and \(U_S(\overline{c})=0\). Now it remains to check, is this BB?

Substituting interim allocation probabilities into the BB condition from above,

\(\displaystyle \mathbb{E}\!\left[p_B(v,c)\right]-\mathbb{E}\!\left[p_S(v,c)\right]\) \(\displaystyle =\) \(\displaystyle \mathbb{E}\!\left[Q_B(v)\,\phi_B(v)\right]-\mathbb{E}\!\left[Q_S(c)\,\phi_S(c)\right]\) \(\displaystyle =\) \(\displaystyle \mathbb{E}\!\left[F_S(v)\left(v-\frac{1-F_B(v)}{f_B(v)}\right)\right]-\mathbb{E}\!\left[\left(1-F_B(c)\right)\left(c+\frac{F_S(c)}{f_S(c)}\right)\right]\) \(\displaystyle =\) \(\displaystyle \int_{\underline{v}}^{\overline{v}} F_S(v)\left(v-\frac{1-F_B(v)}{f_B(v)}\right)f_B(v)\,dv-\int_{\underline{c}}^{\overline{c}} \left(1-F_B(c)\right)\left(c+\frac{F_S(c)}{f_S(c)}\right)f_S(c)\,dc\) \(\displaystyle =\) \(\displaystyle \left[\int_{\underline{v}}^{\overline{v}} F_S(v)\,v\,f_B(v)\,dv-\int_{\underline{v}}^{\overline{v}} F_S(v)\frac{1-F_B(v)}{f_B(v)}f_B(v)\,dv\right]-\left[\int_{\underline{c}}^{\overline{c}} \left(1-F_B(c)\right)c\,f_S(c)\,dc+\int_{\underline{c}}^{\overline{c}} \left(1-F_B(c)\right)\frac{F_S(c)}{f_S(c)}f_S(c)\,dc\right]\) \(\displaystyle =\) \(\displaystyle \int_{\underline{v}}^{\overline{v}} F_S(v)\,v\,f_B(v)\,dv-\int_{\underline{c}}^{\overline{c}} \left(1-F_B(c)\right)c\,f_S(c)\,dc-\int_{\underline{v}}^{\overline{v}} F_S(v)\left(1-F_B(v)\right)dv-\int_{\underline{c}}^{\overline{c}} F_S(c)\left(1-F_B(c)\right)\,dc.\)

Let's simplify the first two terms.

\(\displaystyle \int_{\underline{v}}^{\overline{v}} F_S(v)\,v\,f_B(v)\,dv-\int_{\underline{c}}^{\overline{c}} \left(1-F_B(c)\right)c\,f_S(c)\,dc\) \(\displaystyle =\) \(\displaystyle \mathbb{E}\!\left[V F_S(V)\right]-\mathbb{E}\!\left[C\left(1-F_B(C)\right)\right]\) \(\displaystyle =\) \(\displaystyle \mathbb{E}\!\left[V\,P(C\leq V\mid V)\right]-\mathbb{E}\!\left[C\,P(V\geq C\mid C)\right]\) \(\displaystyle =\) \(\displaystyle \mathbb{E}\!\left[V\,\mathbb{1}(C\leq V)\right]-\mathbb{E}\!\left[C\,\mathbb{1}(C\leq V)\right]\) \(\displaystyle =\) \(\displaystyle \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\!\!\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} v\,\mathbb{1}(c\leq v)f_B(v)f_S(c)\,dv\,dc-\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\!\!\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} c\,\mathbb{1}(c\leq v)f_B(v)f_S(c)\,dv\,dc\) \(\displaystyle =\) \(\displaystyle \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\!\!\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}(v-c)\,\mathbb{1}(c\leq v)\,f_B(v)f_S(c)\,dv\,dc\) \(\displaystyle =\) \(\displaystyle \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\!\!\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left(\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathbb{1}(c\leq x)\,\mathbb{1}(x\leq v)\,dx\right)f_B(v)f_S(c)\,dv\,dc \text{, by the layer-cake representation}\) \(\displaystyle =\) \(\displaystyle \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left(\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathbb{1}(c\leq x)f_S(c)\,dc\right)\left(\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathbb{1}(x\leq v)f_B(v)\,dv\right)dx \text{, by Fubini's Theorem}\) \(\displaystyle =\) \(\displaystyle \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left(\int_{-\infty}^{x}f_S(c)\,dc\right)\left(\int_{x}^{\infty}f_B(v)\,dv\right)dx\) \(\displaystyle =\) \(\displaystyle \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} F_S(x)\left(1-F_B(x)\right)dx.\)

The layer-cake representation for \(v-c\) when \(v\geq c\) is

\[ v-c=\int_c^v dx=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathbb{1}(c\leq x)\,\mathbb{1}(x\leq v)\,dx. \]

Then, substituting back into the earlier BB condition calculation,

\(\displaystyle \mathbb{E}\!\left[p_B(v,c)\right]-\mathbb{E}\!\left[p_S(v,c)\right]\) \(\displaystyle =\) \(\displaystyle \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} F_S(x)\left(1-F_B(x)\right)dx - \int_{\underline{v}}^{\overline{v}} F_S(v)\left(1-F_B(v)\right)dv-\int_{\underline{c}}^{\overline{c}} F_S(c)\left(1-F_B(c)\right)\,dc.\)

First, notice the integrands for the three terms are the same. Then, notice that the integral on the overlapping interval between the two supports \([\underline{v},\overline{v}]\) and \([\underline{c},\overline{c}]\) is subtracted twice from the first integral, which covers the entire real line (importantly, it covers both supports). The three terms cancel, but leave behind one of these subtractions. Since we assumed strictly positive density on both supports, this remaining integral is strictly negative. Thus, the efficient allocation rule is not BB.

Notes

  • Most of the times, the Myerson-Satterthwaite theorem is stated for ex-post BB. Börgers and Norman (2009) show that the two problems are equivalent, that is, if a mechanism is ex-ante BB, then it can be modified to be ex-post BB while preserving IC and IR.
