The Envelope Theorem

A single agent wishes to maximize a real-valued function \(f(x,t)\) with \(t\in[0,1]\) with his choice of \(x\in X\). We call \(f\) the objective function, \(t\) a parameter, and \(x\) the choice variable. Denote the following:

  • \(V(t)=\sup\{f(x,t)\mid x\in X\}\), the value function or upper envelope of the family of functions \(\{f(x,t)\mid x\in X\}\).
  • \(X^*(t)=\operatorname*{arg\,max}\{f(x,t)\mid x\in X\}\), the set of maximizers at \(t\).
  • \(x^*(t)\in X^*(t)\ne\varnothing\), a particular selection from the non-empty set of maximizers at \(t\).

An envelope theorem is one which is concerned with the differentiability of \(V(t)\). There are many different envelope theorems (or perhaps many different versions of the same envelope theorem). We will discuss two that are unique in their generality.

Theorem 1 from Milgrom and Segal (2002) states,

Theorem: Let \(t\in(0,1)\) and suppose \(f_t(x^*(t),t)\) exists. If \(V\) is differentiable at \(t\), then

\[ V'(t)=f_t(x^*(t),t). \]

Theorem 2 from Milgrom and Segal (2002) states,

Theorem: Suppose the following conditions hold:

  1. \(f(x,t)\) is absolutely continuous in \(t\), for all \(x\in X\).
  2. There exists an integrable function \(b:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}_{+}\) such that \(\lvert f_t(x,t)\rvert\le b(t)\) for all \(x\in X\) and almost all \(t\in[0,1]\).

Then, \(V\) is absolutely continuous and, thus, differentiable almost everywhere.

Suppose additionally,

  1. \(f(x,t)\) is differentiable at every \(t\in[0,1]\), for all \(x\in X\).
  2. \(X^*(t)\ne\varnothing\) at almost every \(t\in[0,1]\).

Then, for all \(t\in[0,1]\)

\[ V(t)=V(0)+\int_0^t f_t(x^*(s),s)\,ds. \]
