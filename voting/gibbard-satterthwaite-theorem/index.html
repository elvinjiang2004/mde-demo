The Gibbard-Satterthwaite Theorem

In the voting environment, and give the following result:

Theorem (The Gibbard-Satterthwaite Theorem): When there are three or more candidates, there is no direct voting mechanism that is onto1, not dictatorial, and strategy-proof.

Monotone mechanisms:

Definition: For voter \(i\), for any two of her preference orderings \(\preceq_i\) and \(\preceq_i'\), a candidate \(X\) weakly improves between \(\preceq_i\) and \(\preceq_i'\) if and only if for all candidates \(Y\in E\),

\(Y\preceq_i X \implies Y\preceq_i' X\).

That is, a candidate weakly improves from one preference ordering to another if it is still ranked above all the candidates it was ranked above in the first ordering.

Definition: A direct voting mechanism is monotone if and only if for all candidates \(X\in E\)

\(f(\preceq)=X\) and \(X\) weakly improves for each voter between \(\preceq\) and \(\preceq' \implies f(\preceq')=X\).

That is, a monotone mechanism always retains its elected candidate if that candidate's position in each voter's preference ordering weakly improves.

Lemma 1: If a direct voting mechanism \(f\) is DSIC, then it is monotone.

Proof: Suppose \(f\) is DSIC, but it is not monotone. Then, there exist two distinct preference profiles \(\preceq=(\preceq_1,\ldots,\preceq_n)\), \(\preceq'=(\preceq_1',\ldots,\preceq_n')\) such that \(X=f(\preceq)\neq f(\preceq')=Y\), but candidate \(X\) weakly improves for each voter between \(\preceq\) and \(\preceq'\). Consider the sequence of elected candidates:

\(X=Z_0=f(\preceq)\), \(Z_1=f(\preceq_1',\preceq_2,\ldots,\preceq_n)\), \(\ldots\), \(Z_{n-1}=f(\preceq_1',\ldots,\preceq_{n-1}',\preceq_n)\), \(Y=Z_n=f(\preceq')\).

Since \(X\neq Y\), at some point the sequence of elected candidates switches from \(X\) to a different candidate. Let \(Z_k\) be the candidate the sequence first switches to from \(X\), so \(Z_k\neq X\) and \(Z_{k-1}=X\). By strategy-proofness, \(Z_k\prec_k X\); otherwise, at the reported preference profile \((\preceq_1',\ldots,\preceq_{k-1}',\preceq_k,\ldots,\preceq_n)\), voter \(k\) with preference ordering \(\preceq_k\) can profitably report \(\preceq_k'\) and receive \(Z_k\). Since \(X\) weakly improves for voter \(k\) from \(\preceq_k\) to \(\preceq_k'\), it must also be that \(Z_k\prec_k' X\). But then this mechanism is not DSIC, because at the reported preference profile \((\preceq_1',\ldots,\preceq_k',\preceq_{k+1},\ldots,\preceq_n)\), voter \(k\) with preference ordering \(\preceq_k'\) can instead report \(\preceq_k\) and receive \(X\), which she prefers to \(Z_k\). Thus, \(f\) must be monotone. \(\blacksquare\)

Notes

  1. The requirement that the mechanism be onto does not meaningfully affect the statement from allowing the mechanism's image to be any subset of three or more candidates from the set of candidates. Strategy-proofness ensures that preferences over candidates outside of the mechanism's image cannot affect the outcome and dictatorial-ness would be specified over candidates in the image. The statement is formulated in this way for convenience. ↩
